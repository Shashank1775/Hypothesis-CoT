### Step 2: Solve the system of congruences
To solve the system, we use the method of successive substitutions or the Chinese Remainder Theorem.

#### Step 2.1: Find values of \( m \) that satisfy both conditions
We start with \( m = 9k + 8 \) and substitute it into \( m \equiv 5 \pmod{13} \):
\[
9k + 8 \equiv 5 \pmod{13}.
\]

\[
9k \equiv -3 \pmod{13}.
\]

\[
9k \equiv 10 \pmod{13}.
\]
Next, find the modular inverse of 9 modulo 13. The inverse is the number \( x \) such that \( 9x \equiv 1 \pmod{13} \). Testing values, we find \( x = 3 \) because:
\[
9 \cdot 3 = 27 \equiv 1 \pmod{13}.
\]
Multiply both sides of \( 9k \equiv 10 \pmod{13} \) by 3:
\[
k \equiv 30 \pmod{13}.
\]
Simplify \( 30 \mod 13 \):
\[
k \equiv 4 \pmod{13}.
\]
Thus, \( k = 13n + 4 \) for some integer \( n \).

#### Step 2.2: Substitute \( k \) back into \( m = 9k + 8 \)
Substitute \( k = 13n + 4 \) into \( m = 9k + 8 \):
\[
m = 9(13n + 4) + 8 = 117n + 44.

\[
m = 117(0) + 44 = 44.

#### Case 2: \( n = 1 \)
